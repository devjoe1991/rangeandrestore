When the season changes, your body notices.

*|IF:FNAME|*Hi *|FNAME|*,*|ELSE:|*Hi there,*|END:IF|* the clocks have changed, the evenings are darker, and the body is adjusting to a shift in rhythm. It is one of the more useful moments in the year for a seasonal check-in, particularly if training patterns are changing alongside it.

When the clocks go back, the shift in daylight affects training times, sleep quality, and how much natural movement happens across a day. For many people, it marks a transition from outdoor, varied training to indoor, repetitive training, which carries its own set of soft tissue consequences.

A seasonal session at this point acts as a reset. It addresses what the previous season has left behind and supports the body as it adjusts to a different training environment and cadence.
Seasonal body check

What a seasonal MOT covers

Sessions at this time of year tend to focus on the transition points in the body, the areas that respond most to a change in season or training environment. That typically means the posterior chain after a period of long-distance training, the upper back and neck after a shift to indoor cardio, and the hips and glutes as running gait adapts to heavier winter kit or harder road surfaces.
