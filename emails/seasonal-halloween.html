October is the month when summer training tends to catch up with people. The combination of a busy race season, cooler conditions, and reduced daylight often means the body arrives at autumn carrying more accumulated tightness than it realises.

A well-timed soft tissue session now can clear that before winter training begins and before anything minor becomes something more significant.
Why autumn matters for soft tissue

What the cold actually does to your muscles

When the temperature drops, blood flow to the extremities reduces and muscle tissue contracts slightly. Tendons and fascia become less pliable. The net effect is that the same movement patterns that felt easy in July feel stiffer in October, and small niggles that were manageable become more noticeable.

 Cold weather reduces tissue pliability and slows circulation
